# BLAKE3 pivot v2: inspect the revised success model

Source: `research/blake3_r3/pivotv2/pkg/blake3/experiments/bsrho.py`

Public SHA-256: `856907a5107369b4e3467029fe1f82b625a2cd54860652b051b23259790fb148`

## Reviewed usage contract

This edition revises the probability and batch-planning calculations of pivot1 without changing its hash kernel, stored-length recovery or accounting formulas. It is a concrete example of why a verified circuit is not itself a success-rate proof. The source retains credit to jungjipdo for pivot full addition and the masked walk.

## Source inspection and available execution evidence

static-source-review; Fresh finite arithmetic/allocation and recipe checks; fixture ledger and mocked planner boundaries; no original search or default optimization.

One finite circuit generation and allocation; no planner, probability integral, collision attempt or statistics campaign. The bounded recipe ran with a ten-second timeout.

## Declared inputs

**Target and messages**: Three-round BLAKE3, a single 64-byte message consisting of 32 zero bytes followed by little-endian 256-bit x, standard IV, counter zero and flags 11. The full-width work unit is 638.

**Masked iteration**: The kernel iterates g(x)=f(x) XOR q, with q derived from its output polarities. A fixed q preserves the collision predicate but not the trajectories of the unmasked walk.

**Revised full-width planning**: plan(256) bisects a table-capacity mean and threshold, then calls a batch solver. The loss model uses a 20,000-panel trapezoidal calculation, a 50-step numerical minimization, a 100-step fixed-point iteration and separate failure terms. EPS=0.001, P_MIN=0.390000001, and batches are rounded to multiples of 10^24. These are source-model parameters, not fresh certified probabilities.

**Sensitivity input**: loss(p, rate) forwards rate to a helper that scales L^2/N as a collision-hazard sensitivity. It is not a measured speed multiplier or an independent probability certificate.

**Safe entry point**: Use the compiler-only example. Do not use --selftest, --stats or the default --ledger for a quick documentation check: the first two run actual trials and the last invokes numerical planning and fallback variants.

## Outputs and interpretation

**Fresh finite circuit evidence**: 256 complete-message digest fixtures match the independently pinned reference, with two reference known answers, two fixed masked-walk steps, 18/20-bit fixtures and seven packed recovery lanes. All 17,238 allocated instructions are checked and two intentional operator/register mutations are rejected. Register extent is 771; this is one compiled function.

**Precisely located edition changes**: The AST comparison finds 51 unchanged functions/classes, three changed functions (_plan, plan, loss) and three new helpers (_b256, _lossq, _zq). A complete textual diff was reviewed against the fully read pivot1 source. Shared hash arithmetic does not validate the revised stochastic assumptions.

**Storage and conditional cost**: The unchanged declared layout uses 36-byte instructions and 25-byte endpoint entries with a 64-bit key, 16-bit trail ID and 120-bit stored length. Its body is 17,275 operations, code region 687,060 bytes and table capacity 12,494. Exact ledger summation was checked for an explicitly supplied artificial B=10^35, not a model-selected default budget. Do not publish that fixture numerator as an attack score.

**Reduced-width loss is not a reduced-width success model**: The wrapper forwards Tp, Q/L, capacity, kappa, slice size and rate, but not the input width. Its helper hardcodes L=2^122/Tp and N=2^256. A fresh mocked routing test confirms this boundary. loss(plan(18)) must not be described as an 18-bit success estimate.

**Batch-cache caveat**: The _b256 cache key includes Tp, capacity and kappa but omits Sb, which its computation consumes. A bounded artificial monotone score shows that changing Sb in one process returns the old cached B until the cache is cleared. This is a general-call caveat; it does not show an error for the package’s fixed default slice size.

**Stored-length recovery caveat**: A fresh synthetic noncolliding oracle with stored lengths 1 and capr=1 receives two calls and yields no pair. The loop permits length+1 calls. This documents an edge case, not a real collision or a complete correction to the global recovery budget.

**What was not established**: No full-width default planner, numerical loss integration, original search, new retained-wasm comparison or proof appendix was replayed for this guide. The source comment’s claim that Appendix A proves a probability is an attribution to that appendix, not a result of these tests. Float bisection also does not alone prove global optimality or the least feasible integer budget.

## Failure modes

- Do not promote floating loss estimates or mocked planner controls to exact success certificates.
- Do not reuse the full-width loss helper as an 18/20-bit probability model.
- Changing Sb requires invalidating the batch cache; the key does not include it.
- A fixed supplied B gives an exact arithmetic ledger, not evidence that its probability target is met.
- Stored-length recovery may make one extra no-hit oracle call; this guide does not reprice the entire attack.
- Circuit equality across editions does not transfer retained-wasm execution evidence or probability proofs.
