**Limits of the evidence.**

- H1 is an assumption: the factor 49,400,000 is set against a count under M; the
  count does not show that M holds, nor does M give the second half of H1.
- M fails inside one context (rule A) and one outer step (E1), holding only on
  average over outer steps; whether good trials (rule A and R = 0) keep its rate
  on average cannot be measured: nothing reaches R = 0 at 32 bits.
- The model is checked on real messages only to about 2^-40, not 2^-100.53; in
  toy collision runs the largest (no sub-class or rule) is 3.3 percent low at 8
  bits (2.65 standard deviations), level at 10, the smaller with both level.
- Choosing constants, class, sub-class and rule by the count says nothing about
  the model and favours any choice it overrates; the 1.142 sub-class gain is
  unmeasured (without it: 3.28, not 3.75, times the assumed factor).
- The count rests on four betas with 98.8%, a 60-beta list whose completeness
  rests on uncertified solver answers and the other model's enumeration, and
  unpackaged programs; the second count, another AI model's, was compared file
  by file, not read line by line by a person, not blind.
- Trials are assumed independent though sharing four seed words per run, six per
  outer step of 2^49 (for one member also the table row), Y12 and w5 over
  2,222,477 * 2^32 trials; a context's 131,072 differ only via Y4.
- H2 and H3 concern run totals: the stage-2 batch share, measured three times on
  2^30 trials and by the review in step 2's ranges, has 19 percent room below
  the budget in these samples, but single contexts exceed it, cause unanalysed;
  hitting a budget is failure, lowering success probability, not the time bound.
- The loops, their ranges and step 3 exist only in this text; the program counts
  their pieces, and loop control and an E1 test pass are bounded in words.
- The batch count is the program's own on a machine keeping nine words in
  registers; 6.5 gives it without that.
- H4 and H5 are of a kind this participant has not filed before. H4 rests on a
  mean (time proportional to 1 / (1 + 40.02)) measured three times to one part
  in a thousand, scatter about 14 per cent, on random outer words, not 6.4's
  walk; a yield one part in ten lower adds about 0.15, one of 1 gives 95.7.
- Clusters, flips and gate were chosen on a sampler; searching 231,525 flip sets
  favours one that happens to look good; three programs remeasured it on much
  larger samples; another AI model's re-screen checks only the screen.
- The neighbour-path budget, 1.2 times the measured shares, is as unproved as
  step 4's; a sample's top X2 value is no bound and grows with it (1.64 times
  the mean over 8,192, 1.72 over 16,384); charging it gives 93.83 and 94.14.
- H5's first part is measured to 0.04% and 0.3% on two sides, the other model's
  exact values on three partial averages within 0.8% of one; none is proof, the
  ratios are dependent, all are collision proxies. Its no-clumping part is
  measured only at 2^-9 and 2^-19, about 100 bits short; an opened cluster
  firing as one trial adds about 1.3 to 1.8, most of the cluster gain.
- Section 8's neighbour-path charge is thus a lower bound (pieces counted
  separately); its two audits, by other AI models, unread line by line by a
  person, differ by 0.18 per trial-equivalent.
- Spare lanes: a list's last word (6.5) has three lanes repeating a member; a
  repeated representative lane would open a cluster twice, which Lemma C does
  not cover; the other model's partition suppresses the gate and rule A on three
  lanes outside the sub-class at 4 units per X2 value, a relative 3 in 16,384.
- The cluster layout, seven planes of 133,413 words in the other model's audit,
  is about eight times the five lists and table of 6.4, about 30 megabytes,
  within memory_log2_bytes = 35 but above Section 9's search-alone figure.
- Helper agents of the participant wrote and checked the lemmas and this text;

**Approaches tried that did not improve this path.** For this family only.

- Solving the residual inside-out with redundancy: complete known sets start at
  redundancy two on one side (222,513, none with a split equation), are absent
  at two on another (three unfinished) and up to four for all seven words; at
  2^32 per unit it needs redundancy one to pay. Proposed by the participant.
- A SAT solver per trial forcing further conditions: 0.6 to 6.0 seconds at
  levels 0 to 2, no answer in 900 seconds at levels 3 and 4, against about seven
  operations per trial. Proposed by the participant; run by a helper agent.
- Forcing a second condition via a coupled block of equations: thirty of thirty
  cases unsatisfiable; smallest forcing blocks 35, 20 and 49 equations (one test
  word, the other, both); the heaviest cube costs at least 18,900 operations per
  forced trial against break-even 11,673, at least 0.7 bit lost. Helper agents.
- Bit-level steering and neutral bits, first-stage test: no neutral bit or
  zero-loss single-bit skip exists; a free-bit flip changes the five-condition
  rule of entry c66f230d with probability 0.035 to 0.063 and leaves rule A
  unchanged in at most 2 times 2^-8; no deeper word steers cheaply. The study
  found the neighbour effect of 6.5. Proposed by the participant; helper agents.
- Table lookups fixing a second word, and deeper loops: no gain; per-trial
  equations stay at the two-level numbers (11, 25 and 26 to the first test word,
  the second, both; 6, 18 and 19 for entry c66f230d). Helper agents.
- Other absolute values of the pinned constants: the best of 4,432 realizations
  beats the count in use, 162,266,764, by 177.69, about one part in a million.
  From an observation of GPT Sol 6.1; helper agents.
- Four-active fork: best complete trail 253 against 100; costs proved

- Other message-length families: the best are estimated at 101.3 to 107, not

- Two "knob" words fixing first-stage bits: false on 2,000 real trials. Proposed

**Scope and limitations.**

- No full collision is exhibited; the search is an analytical cost claim like
  other packages on this track and, unlike a birthday search, tests each trial
  against zero, needing no memory that grows with the trials.
- Messages are 55 and 63 bytes; the construction relies on the true block length
  being a compression input (target profile); the longer ends in eight zero
  bytes; both zero-filled blocks have words 14, 15 and word 13's top byte zero.
- Versus a birthday search: half the digest matched by construction (no table,
  about half the round-1 work); constants letting the rest match usefully; a Y4
  sub-class assumed at 49,400,000 times the uniform rate; work shared per outer
  step or X2 value. Trials: 2^101.442, not 2^128 (2^127 at the uniform rate).
- A brief literature search found free-start collisions and near-collisions of
  reduced BLAKE compression functions and no collision attack on 2-round BLAKE3.
  No priority or novelty claim is made.
- The time bound counts arithmetic, logical and shift operations of the four
  pieces, comparisons, branches and every load and store of 6.5's machine, stage
  2 at the share step 4 allows: an upper bound under that convention, not a
  measured time, rerunnable via self-test (participant only; no organizer run).
