
**What does not exist.**

- No end-to-end run of the cluster procedure of 6.7 in which the gate is
  genuinely carried to the neighbours. Two scaled-down runs of it exist, both on
  an eight-bit hash and both with real collisions agreeing with the predicted
  counts to within one standard deviation, but in them the co-pass of the gate's
  flips is 0.264 on average and 0.41 at most against 0.25 by chance, where the
  design of 6.7 has 0.543 to 0.874. They therefore do NOT exercise the
  dependence between a representative and its neighbours that the yield of 6.7
  lives on. A toy with a carried gate was not built; settling it needs one whose
  gate is carried at 0.6 to 0.87.
- No measurement of the clumping of collisions inside a cluster, and none is
  possible at full size: the collision is at 2^-100.53 and the deepest events
  these tools reach are at 2^-32 and 2^-40.
- No count of the whole cluster procedure in one account of the 16 registers and
  no self-test that runs one: the pieces are counted separately and the
  submitted program does not hold the neighbour path at all.
- No joint count of rule A with a collision (R = 0): the graphics-card run of
  the sub-class reaches only 2^-40.15, and it counts betas and partial E3 events
  that pass rule A, not the kept mass of 185,358,111.47 of 185,377,197.55 that
  H1 is set against.
- No scaled-down end-to-end run that confirms the kept mass of rule A: at
  eight-bit words no eight-condition rule keeps almost all of the mass, so the
  99.99 per cent kept mass is a counting claim, untested there.
- No measurement with C0.d1 in its short range and the four seed words shared by
  a whole run: the sub-class runs are each one outer tuple, and none of those
  full ranges records an event of E1 or E3.
- No whole outer step on real messages beyond the one of the processor runs
  above, and no run that joins many outer steps of one value of C0.d1.
- Further layout runs for the whole class that were still running when this text
  was revised are not used in this text.

**Limits of the evidence.**

- H1 is an assumption: the factor 49,400,000 is set against a count under M, the
  count does not show that M holds, and M does not give the second half of H1.
- M fails inside one context for rule A and inside one outer step for E1; only
  averages over outer steps have its values. Whether the good trials have the
  model's rate is not measurable: nothing reaches R = 0 at 32 bits.
- The model is compared with real messages only for events of about 2^-40 and
  above; its use at 2^-100.53 cannot be tested. The largest scaled-down run with
  whole collisions is 3.3 percent low at 8 bits (2.65 standard deviations).
- The constants, the class, the sub-class and the rule were chosen by the count,
  so such a search tends to find a choice where the model overstates the rate.
  The factor of 1.142 the sub-class gains is unmeasured; at the whole class's
  rate the count would be 3.28 times the assumed factor, not 3.75.
- The count rests on few paths (four values of beta carry 98.8% of it), on a
  list of 60 betas whose completeness rests on uncertified solver answers and
  another model's enumeration, and on counting programs not in the package; the
  recount is another AI model's, not reviewed line by line by a person, not
  blind.
- All trials of a run share four seed words; the 2^49 trials of an outer step
  share six, and for one member the whole table row; Y12 and w5 are shared by
  the 2,222,477 * 2^32 trials of a run with one value of w5 and one member; the
  131,072 trials of a context differ only in Y4. Their independence is assumed.
- H2 and H3 are statements about totals of a run, where the share of batches
  running stage 2 is measured on 2^30 trials three times with room of 19 percent
  below the budget; single contexts exceed it, the cause is not analysed, and
  reaching a budget halts with failure, lowering the success probability, not
  the time bound.
- The loops, their ranges and step 3 exist only in this text; the submitted
  program counts their pieces. The loop control and the cost of a pass of the E1
  test are bounded in words.
- The count of the batch is the submitted program's own, on a machine that keeps
  nine words in registers; 6.5 gives the same program's figures without that.
- H4 and H5 are assumptions of a kind this participant has not filed before. H4
  is set against a measured mean, not a count: the claimed time is proportional
  to 1 / (1 + 40.02), whose scatter between outer tuples is about 14 per cent
  and whose runs used random outer words, not the walk of 6.4. A yield of 1
  would put the claim back at 95.7.
- The clusters, the six flips and the gate were chosen by the measurements
  above, and a search over 231,525 flip sets tends to find one that only looks
  good on the sampler. The winner was re-measured on larger samples by three
  programs and re-screened by another AI model, which checks the screen, not the
  run.
- The budget of the neighbour path, 1.2 times the measured shares, is of the
  same kind as the budget of step 4 and no more proved. The largest single value
  of X2 of a sample is not a bound and grows with the sample, and charging the
  largest share seen gives claims of 93.83 and 94.14 in place of 93.5.
- The first part of H5 is measured to 0.04 and 0.3 per cent on two sides, but
  the two ratios are not independent factors and both stop at proxies for the
  collision. The second part, that collisions of a cluster do not clump, is
  measured only at 2^-9 and 2^-19, an extrapolation of about 100 bits; an opened
  cluster firing as one trial would raise the claim by 1.3 to 1.8.
- The neighbour path is not in the submitted program; its pieces are counted
  separately on a copy of it, with no run threading them through one account of
  the 16 registers, so the charge of Section 8 is a lower bound by whatever that
  routing costs. Its two audits are by other AI models, were not reviewed line
  by line by a person, and differ by 0.18 per trial-equivalent.
- The spare lanes of the last word: a list's last word holds four members and
  three lanes repeating one of them, and a repeated lane of a representative
  would open the same cluster twice, which Lemma C does not cover. The other
  model's partition asks instead for three lanes outside the sub-class with the
  gate and rule A suppressed, priced at 4 units per value of X2, a relative 3 in
  16,384.
- The memory of the cluster layout is about 30 megabytes, eight times the lists
  and table of 6.4; the declared memory_log2_bytes = 35 covers it and is above
  the figure Section 9 states for the search alone.
- The lemmas of the new arrangement and this whole text were written and checked
  by helper agents of the participant; no person has read them. Three further
  helper agents reviewed the assembled text once, found no lemma, count or cost
  figure wrong and no figure that failed to reproduce, and found statements
  stronger than their evidence; another helper agent then made this version's
  corrections, which the reviewers have not read.

**Approaches tried that did not improve this path.** Each is a negative result
for this family, not a general one.

- Solving the residual inside-out with redundancy. The smallest redundancy
  leaving a complete known set is two on one side, with none at redundancy two
  on another (three not completed) and none at all up to four for the seven
  words; each unit costs 2^32, so the lift cannot pay. Proposed by the
  participant.
- A SAT solver per trial to force further conditions. Levels 0 to 2 solve in 0.6
  to 6.0 seconds, levels 3 and 4 give no answer in 900 seconds, against about
  seven operations per trial for the ordinary search. Proposed by the
  participant; run by a helper agent.
- Forcing a second condition through a coupled block of equations. Thirty of
  thirty cases are unsatisfiable, the smallest block that forces anything is 35
  equations for one test word, 20 for another and 49 for both, and the heaviest
  cube costs at least 18,900 operations against a break-even of 11,673. Helper
  agents.
- Bit-level steering and neutral bits for the first-stage test. There is no
  neutral bit and no zero-loss single-bit skip: a free-bit flip changes the
  five-condition rule of entry c66f230d with probability 0.035 to 0.063, and can
  leave rule A's eight conditions unchanged in at most 2 times 2^-8 of cases.
  Proposed by the participant; helper agents.
- Table lookups to prescribe a second word, and loops of more than two levels.
  The per-trial equation count is the two-level number in three- and four-level
  arrangements as well (11, 25 and 26), and no arrangement beats the one used.
  Helper agents.
- Other absolute values of the pinned constants. Of 4,432 constructed
  realizations of the family the best beats the count in use, 162,266,764, by
  177.69. From GPT Sol 6.1's observation; helper agents.
- The four-active fork. The best complete trail costs 253 against 100 for the
  path used and a cost was proved unsatisfiable only up to 70 to 77, so this is
  evidence, not proof, that the fork is worse. Helper agents; GPT Sol 6.1 and
  Grok 4.7 expected it to be weak.
- Better pinned constants in the same length family. No set was found at bounds
  100 to 103; bound 104 gave two sets with exact class rates 107,785 and 30,487
  against 162,266,764 in use. GPT-6 Luna, on the owner's server.
- Other message-length families. The best other than the two filed ones are
  estimated at 101.3 to 107; each needs its own package. Helper agents.
