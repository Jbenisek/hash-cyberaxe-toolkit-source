**Real messages in the arrangement of this package: what exists.** All of the
following are participant measurements.

*The self-test and the equivalence runs (processor).* The self-tests of 6.5
compare every lane with the complete digests of its two real messages: 7,000,000
lanes in the long run, and 200,000 batches decided as a plain first form does.
One complete outer step was run: all 18,725 table words equal the words of their
members, and 18,725 batches of one value of X2 (131,075 lanes) and 3,000 of each
of three more are right against the complete digests.

*Rule A and the entry to stage 2 in the sub-class (processor).* Three runs of
2^30 real trials each, two of them drawn from the same seed text and so
overlapping. Rule A is satisfied by 2^-8.0019, 2^-7.9992 and 2^-8.0000 of the
trials. The share of the batches that run stage 2 is 0.02617, 0.02622 and
0.02622; independent trials would give 0.0270, and the budget is 0.03125. Three
helper agents, each with its own program, measured the same share again at
0.02617 to 0.02620, with a few single contexts above the budget. Until the
graphics-card and scaled-down runs below, these were the only measurements that
have the sub-class and the rule of this package.

*Layout runs of the two-level arrangement (graphics card).* Real messages in the
layout of step 2, but for the whole class of 524,288 members and with the rules
of four, five and six conditions of entry c66f230d: 24 outer tuples of 2^40
trials, two of 2^44 and 4,096 consecutive values of w5, recounted by a second
helper agent. Averaged over outer tuples the counts agree with the model within
their statistical error; for the deepest event that error is large, 138 of 152.0
being a ratio of 0.91 with one-sided 95% limits 0.785 and 1.046.

| listed beta | 2^-7.96 | +0.73 standard deviations |

What they do not support, and what this text must not claim: independence of the
trials inside one outer step. The share of the trials of one value of X2 that
satisfy a rule is not binomial (for five conditions its variance is 2.7 to 181
times binomial), and the members of a batch pass together. For E1 the rate of a
listed tau given a listed beta is a property of the outer tuple: for 17 cells
carrying 24% of the count it lies between 0.02 and 2 times its average, and it
is the same for 4,096 consecutive values of w5, because Y12 and w5 are fixed in
an outer step. A weighted estimate of one outer tuple's rate of solutions has a
standard deviation of about 0.7% between tuples; it has mean 1 by construction
and stops at beta and tau. One count is unexplained: in one window of 2^44
trials of one tuple, 3 listed E1 outcomes were seen where 14.65 are expected
(probability 0.0003 under a Poisson law), the next window of the same tuple has
16, and the cells one level up do not differ. Chance is the likelier reading and
it is not excluded that it is not chance. The E1 test passes at 2^-31.41 per
trial (2^-31.20 in the tuple with the most). The outer words here are random: no
run has C0.d1 in the short range of step 2, and none holds a whole outer step,
the longest having 2^26 values of X2 of one tuple.

*Scaled-down end-to-end runs in the two-level order.* The whole search was run
on small versions of the hash, 8-bit and 10-bit words, every trial hashed in
full with no rule and no filter, against the exact solution count of the
seven-word system. A job with its expected values and judgement rule written
down beforehand: 8,448 runs, 8,108 collisions against 8,313.6 predicted (-2.25
standard deviations); with 8-bit words 6,276 against 6,489.6 in 6,528 runs, 3.3
per cent low (-2.65). The first job of 92 runs in that sum was low on its own
(56 against 74.8, -2.17); two later pre-declared jobs were not. By the judgement
rule only a deviation of more than 3 standard deviations below the prediction
would speak against the model. No run has a failed check or a false collision.
The cause of the shortfall at 8 bits is not known; a dependence of the rate of
one outer step on its fixed words, stronger at small word sizes, is one possible
reading and is not established. These runs have no sub-class, no rule A and no
packed batch.

*Scaled-down end-to-end runs in the order of entry c66f230d.* The same test in
that entry's order: 1,272 runs in a plan fixed beforehand, 345 collisions
against 335.76 predicted (+0.50 standard deviations); in a second plan one row
has 2 runs without a collision where 0.25 are expected (probability 0.024), and
24 further runs made to look at it have 93 against 93.97.

*Real messages for the sub-class with rule A (graphics card).* The sub-class and
rule A were run on real messages: 24 outer tuples, 2^23 values of X2 each with
all 131,072 members, 2^44.59 trials in all, bad 0 and contradictions 0. Rule A
passes at 2^-8.000002 of the trials (103,079,088,429 passes), and 0.026178 of
the packed words of seven consecutive members enter stage 2, against 0.027025
for independent members and 0.03125 charged, so the measured entry is 0.838 of
what the package charges. The deepest events with an exact probability are the
listed partial E3 event at 2^-32.2 (6,314 seen against 5,353.63 on the
whole-class value) and the listed E1 outcome at 2^-40.15 (14 seen against 21.59,
a shortfall this run does not resolve). On the same pin and geometry the listed
partial E3 event comes at 1.18 times its whole-class rate, 8.9 standard
deviations of the run scatter apart. It is NOT the counted gain: the marker is
one call deep at 2^-32 while the gain 1.142 is in the counted rate r at
2^-100.53, which no measurement can reach; and rule A is a necessary condition
for a collision and so is positively correlated with the marker (268 of the
6,314 partial E3 events pass rule A, a factor of 10.9 over the rule's own rate),
which is not evidence that the rule keeps the counted mass. The kept mass of
rule A, 185,358,111.47 of 185,377,197.55, is not measured by it.

*A scaled-down end-to-end run with the sub-class and an eight-condition rule.*
The whole search was run on an eight-bit version of the hash with both pieces
this package adds: a sub-cube keeping a quarter of the members (two more fixed
bits of e1) and an eight-condition parity rule on h1 (pass rate 2^-8), every
passing trial hashed in full as two real messages of 55 and 63 toy bytes. In 30
runs, 515,396,075,520 trials examined and 2,013,236,457 passing the rule and
hashed: 121 collisions against 120.23 predicted from the exact table over the
trials tried (standard deviation 10.96, z +0.07), with bad 0, bogus 0 and lost
0. The plan and reading rule were hash-stamped before the first six counted
runs; the other twenty-four after that first result (29 against 24.05) was read.
This establishes, with real collisions, that a sub-cube condition on e1 and an
eight-condition rule on h1 leave the survivors' collision rate at the exact
per-trial count, within 9 per cent. It does NOT establish the kept mass of rule
A: at eight-bit words no eight-condition rule keeps almost all of the collision
mass (the scaled rule keeps 7 to 9 per cent, where the real rule keeps 99.99 per
cent by the count), so that part of H1 stays a counting claim, untested here.

*Chosen and random outer words for the sub-class (graphics card).* The sub-class
was also run with the fixed outer words chosen so that the model predicts more
listed E1 outcomes (good), chosen for fewer (bad), and random. The good arm has
421 listed E1 outcomes against the model's 421.2 and an average over outer steps
of 345.7, the bad arm 235 against 226.1 and an average of 288.1, the random arm
275 against 288.1; the good and bad arms differ by 5.04 standard deviations. The
disclosure it carries: single outer steps of the sub-class differ from the
average over outer steps by about plus or minus 8.48 per cent in the model's
weighted rate (the chosen arms measured 1.0980 and 0.9115 times the random arm),
and random outer steps by about 3.7 to 4.0 per cent from one step to the next,
while the claim uses the average over outer steps. It is a measurement at a
2^-40 marker for the whole-class count; it reaches no collision and does not
measure the factor.
