# A6: per-frame tables by their true key (ANGLES.md section 6), 2026-10-09

**Verdict: CLOSED.** The whole per-frame table never repeated: 0 duplicates in 2^20 random frames and 0 in 2^20 frames where
only w6 moves. A key with 2^31.3 values would have given about 200 (DERIVED in ANGLES). The planted control was caught exactly.

## What the per-frame table is (read from pkg16f halfsearch.py: ctr_outer, ctr_table_word, ctr_batch, ctr_lane)
- The member table `ctr_table_word` (ROL(E1.d1,16), E1.d1 for the 2^21 cube values c1) is the same for the whole run, so it
  does not depend on the frame. The per-frame part is what `ctr_batch` computes for all 2^21 members from the frame's
  memory words. Those words are Y12, Q = -Y1-w12, C2.b1, C2.c1, w5 (and w5+delta), and V = rule_word(Y4), which depends
  only on e1 = Y3+Y4. Per member the batch gives (a) stage A, the rule-A flag on h1 = ROR(Y14 ^ e1, 16), and (b) the
  32-bit E1 word (zero means a hit).
- The table hashed here is all 2^21 members x (word, rule-A flag). Each is hashed as two 64-bit position-keyed sums (128 bits)
  for the word part and two more for the rule-A part. Equal tables always give equal hashes. Hash collisions are
  below 2^-88 over all pairs.

## Checks (all MEASURED)
- The lane formula (gen_frames.lane) matched ctr_lane, the full pair of 2-round compressions, on 280 of 280 lanes (40 frames x 7).
- The GPU against Python on two whole tables, all 2^21 members each: frame 0 (no rule-A passes) and frame 564 (8,912 passes,
  1 joint hit). All 7 fields matched: 4 hash sums and 3 counts.
- Positive control: 4,096 frames, each with two planted twins (Y12^2^31, Q+2^31, w5+2^31) and (C2.b1^2^31, C2.c1+2^31).
  The run found 8,192 whole-table duplicates with largest group 3, which is exactly what was expected. So these two top-bit
  symmetries are real (DERIVED, now MEASURED) but remove only 2 bits.

## Side finding (stage A alone; MEASURED, outside this angle)
- 32.4% of frames have an empty stage-A table: no member of the 2^21 passes rule A (339,867 random, 341,598 w6). The mean is
  8,213 passes per frame, so a non-empty frame has about 12,150 on average.
- The non-empty stage-A tables are highly structured. Pass counts are multiples of 1,024 (16,384 and 32,768 are the most
  common). Their repeats sit almost entirely at 16,384 and 32,768: 74 + 45 of 145 groups. GUESS: that class has an effective key
  near 2^20 per pass-count class, but most stage-A tables are unique. So a stage-A lookup does not cover the run.
- Lead for angle 5 (early abort), untested: if an empty frame can be recognised in O(1), the 32.4% dead frames stop costing
  stage A. That is worth about 0.57 bits at most (DERIVED from 1/0.676), and only if the cost model counts those frames.
