## 3. Operation ledger

### 3.1 Machine
Machine model declared by this entry (as in 3c2d1220 and 0a9640ed, which passed the judges with it). The benchmark's cost model reads (collision-frontier-v5, `cost_model`): "Classical probabilistic 256-bit word RAM. One selected-round target compression or sponge permutation costs one unit; every other primitive word operation costs 1/C units"; its primitive operations are "256-bit load or store", "addition/subtraction modulo 2^256", "bitwise AND/OR/XOR/NOT", "shift or rotation", "comparison", "conditional branch", "independent uniform random 256-bit word"; its memory includes "code", "working state", "tables". It fixes the word size and prices loads and stores, and it gives no register count.
This entry therefore declares a load/store machine with 256-bit words, 256-bit immediates and R = 800 registers (a declared convention, H3, supporting). Charged: every addition, AND, OR, XOR, shift, comparison, conditional branch and the one random word; every load and every store of a word, one operation each; every register move, one operation each.
The main loop has no loads, stores or moves: the generated straight line needs 773 registers (`allocate` assigns them; output plane j is written into the register that held input plane j, so the next batch reads the same registers), leaving 27 of the 800 for the loop counter, the limit and the handlers' temporaries. The file of 800 registers is counted as memory: 25,600 bytes (Section 7).
Constants are immediates. The organizer's reference count C(r3) = 638 (14 + 208 r) is used only as the unit: Unit = 638 operations. Code is memory, and the cost model does not fix its format; this entry prices it. A gate of the step, of the stage-1 test or of the recovery circuit (XOR, AND or OR of two registers into a register) is one 40-bit instruction word, charged 5 bytes: an 8-bit opcode and three 10-bit register numbers (800 registers are below 1,024). The generator writes exactly these records (Section 3.6). Every other instruction (the compares and branches of the stage-1 test, loop control, handlers, setup, cut and slice, the recovery besides its circuit, and the WebAssembly image of the generator) is charged 36 bytes (opcode, registers and an inline 256-bit immediate). The 5-byte word is a price stated by this entry (H3), not a measurement.

### 3.2 One batch = 16563 operations for 256 trails
Representation: a 32-bit word is 32 planes; plane j is a 256-bit integer whose bit l is bit j of the word of lane l. A rotation is a renaming of planes (no operation). XOR of words is 32 gates. Constants (the IV words, the prefix words m0..m7, the length and the flag word) are folded at build time. Each signal is a pair (node, polarity): the register holds the node's value and the true plane is that value XOR polarity, so complements are free.
Two-addend sums (`Gen.add`) are a ripple of full adders. The full adder is the pivot adder of 6ae36351 (`Gen.fa`): of three variable inputs two have the same polarity; with the third as the pivot p and the two equal ones x, y: u = x XOR p, sum = u XOR y, carry = (u AND (y XOR p)) XOR p; u and y XOR p have equal polarity, so the AND is one gate (an AND of two upright planes or an OR of two inverted ones): 5 gates. With one constant input k: sum = x XOR y XOR k, carry = x AND y (k = 0) or x OR y (k = 1). For k = 1 and x, y of opposite stored polarity the carry is built as yy OR (x XOR y), yy the operand whose polarity equals that of x XOR y, which reuses the sum's XOR: 2 gates (the constant-bit carry fold suggested by Grok, xAI, checked here by the selftest and the reference comparison). With two constants the plane passes through.
Three-addend sums (`Gen.add3`, the column of 10ce9f59). BLAKE3 computes a = a + b + m twice per G call. When all 96 planes of a, b and m are variable (20 times at three rounds; the first half of round 0 and every sum with a prefix word are partly constant and use two two-addend ripples), the three words are added in one ripple whose carry between columns is a pair of planes (u, U) standing for the value U ? 1 : 2u. Bit 0 is a pivot full adder whose carry enters as the pair (0, k). A column takes the three addend bits x, y, z and the incoming pair and returns the sum bit and the outgoing pair, x + y + z + c = s + 2 c':
- template 0: n5 = z ^ U, n9 = x ^ n5, s = y ^ n9, n6 = x ^ y, n8 = U | (z ^ u), n11 = !n6 & n9, u' = n5 ^ n11, U' = n8 ^ n11;
- template 1: w = y ^ U, n8 = z ^ w, s = x ^ n8, n6 = U | (y ^ u), u' = n6 ^ w, U' = n6 ^ ((z ^ w) & (x ^ w));
- form 2: for the four stored-polarity contexts (x, y, z, u, U polarities 16 or 7 in the program's encoding, with or without u') in which both templates need 10 gates, a fixed circuit of AND, OR and XOR gates on the stored planes from an exact synthesis (table `COLLIB` of the program; each checked on all 32 input rows, and by the selftest through the whole step).
For each of bits 1 to 30 the generator builds the six addend orders with the three forms as dry trials (the new gates are removed again) and keeps the first with the fewest new gates; bit 30 builds only U'; bit 31 is s = x ^ y ^ z ^ U (3 gates). At three rounds every such sum costs 5 + 8 + 28 * 9 + 8 + 3 = 276 gates (two two-addend ripples: 308): the census of the 600 columns of bits 1 to 30 is 560 middle columns of 9 gates (403 template 0, 145 template 1, 12 form 2) and 40 boundary columns of 8 gates (24 template 0, 13 template 1, 3 form 2).
The generator builds the straight line at run time. No two gates of the network are equal and every gate is used (the reference generator `Gen`/`Step`, which would share equal gates and drop unused ones, finds none: the selftest prints 0 shared and 0 unused gates), so the machine generator of Section 3.6 builds the network in two passes and keeps no node table.
The mask walk: the circuit's 256 output planes are stored in registers exactly as the circuit produces them and are the 256 input planes of the next batch, so no operation complements a plane and the chain is x -> g(x) = f(x) XOR q. Equality under g is equivalent to equality under f (Section 1).
The generated line (`python3 bsrho.py --ledger`, `--selftest`): 16546 gates = 13490 XOR + 1320 AND + 1736 OR = **16546 operations**, no complement operation. Per trail 64.63 operations = 0.1013 unit, against 1 unit for a compression.
The generator runs once at the start of the attempt; its work is charged in the setup row (Section 3.3), its code and peak storage in Sections 3.6 and 7.

### 3.3 Loop, stage 1, handlers and the other events
Loop body = one batch: 16546 + stage-1 test 14 + loop control 3 = 16563 operations. Stage 1 (`stage1`, `stage1_ops`): the 13 lowest planes of digest word 0 (stored planes, upright: they are the planes of the next point) are ORed (12 ORs), the result is compared with the all-ones immediate and a branch ends the batch when it is all ones: 14 operations. On a passing batch (some lane is zero on the 13 planes) the other 19 planes are ORed into it, compared with all ones and branched on: 21 more operations. A lane is a stage-1 hit when its 32 low bits are zero; the complement of the OR that marks the hits is formed in the hit handler. The test loses no event: a DP has all 32 low bits zero, so its batch passes and its lane is a hit. Passing batches are counted and halt the run beyond pmax = ceil(2 B P13) + 64 (Lemma 2, Step 7); they are charged at the budget: pmax * 21.
Loop control: batch counter increment, compare with the next slice limit, branch back: 3 operations.
Handlers (charged per event at the stated bounds; instruction sequences use the same registers):
- Stage-1 hit: the complement of the mask (1), store the 256 planes to an 8 KiB copy in memory (256 stores), then per plane: load, shift right by the lane number, AND 1, shift left by the plane number, OR into the accumulator, address increment, plane counter increment, compare, branch (9 per plane, 2,304), find the lane in the mask (at most 8 halving steps of 5), window test of F < Tp (3 extractions and at most 12 further operations), counters. Total below 2,800; charged **2,800** per hit. The hit budget e2max is enforced by a halt.
- Finished trail (a DP, new start): key formation, the new start r + t' (1 add), insertion of the 256 bits of the new start in the lane: per plane load, shift, AND 1, shift left by the lane number, AND with the complement of the lane bit, OR, store, address and counter increments, compare, branch (12 per plane, 3,072), reload of the 256 planes (256), lane arrays and counters (below 200). Total below 3,550; charged **3,800**. The table walk is charged separately per slot of the table: a lookup visits at most 3 S slots (the walk back to the start of the cluster, the walk forward over the runs, the scan of the key's run; S = tmax + 1 slots) and an insertion moves at most S entries one slot to the right; the 56-bit slot i lies at bit 56 i and spans at most two words, so a visit is at most 12 operations (offset arithmetic 3, 2 loads, 2 shifts, OR, mask, test, branch, increment) and a move at most 24 (a read and a two-word read-modify-write); charged **64 (S)** per finished trail (3 * 12 + 24 = 60 <= 64). At most tmax finished trails (a halt on a full table).
- Slice check (every 2^48 batches): the 256 births (load, subtract, compare, branch: 1,024), slice counter: charged **1,300** each. Cut trail: insertion of the new start as above plus bookkeeping, charged **4,200**; at most 1 cut trail (a halt on the second).
- Recovery. A pass-1 batch: the packed circuit on two lanes (609 operations, the last 8 of them the XORs with q), then the loop increment, compare and branch (3), call, return and the 8 moves of the digest (10), the DP test of each of the two lanes (low 32 bits: shift, AND, branch; window: shift, AND, compare, branch; 7 per lane, 14), the two finished flags (6), and the checkpoint countdown (decrement, compare, branch: 3): **609 + 36**. A stored checkpoint pair: per lane the 256-bit point is extracted from the 8 packed words (shift, AND, shift, OR per word: 32), stored (1) and its address advanced (1); with the countdown reset, **80** per pair, at most 8,192 pairs. A pass-2 batch: the packed circuit plus the equality test of lane 0 and lane 1 on the 8 output words (8 shifts, 8 XORs, 7 ORs, 1 AND with the lane-0 mask, 1 compare, 1 branch = 26), loop 3, call 2 and 8 moves: **609 + 39** (a single-lane walk of a probe needs no test and is inside the same charge). Pass 2 runs at most 16 single-lane walks of at most D - 1 batches (the probe of P(0), at most ceil(log2(8,193)) = 14 bisection probes, and the walk to A_(lo D + d)) and one joint walk of at most D batches: at most 17 D batches. A probe besides its walk (midpoint, two checkpoint addresses and loads, insertion into lane 0 of the 8 packed words 24, extraction 32, compare, branch, update, loop): below 100, charged **100**, 16 probes. Call, d = l1 - l2, the quotient and remainder of d by D (13-bit quotient), the final walk length and the output: **600**. Pass 1 is bounded by its halt at c_r; pass 2 by the bisection.
- Setup and generator: 1 random word; the 256 starts r + t inserted in memory (256 x 3,100 = 793,600, each at most 12 x 256 + 28) and one reload; counters; fixed part charged 813,600. The generator: 491,076 primitive calls (two passes of 245,538 gate-constructor calls each, the dry trials of the three-addend columns included) and 16546 emitted lines, each charged 200 word operations: 101,524,400. Setup and generator together 102,338,000 operations.
- Choice of the prefix K (offline, once; Section 3.7): 12,658,758,600 operations, charged in the time bound.
Code: 17,186 gate words (the step 16546, the 31 ORs of stage 1, the recovery circuit 609) and 1,337 other instructions (loop control 3, the compares and branches of stage 1 4, handlers 1,060: 1,000 for the 256 stores, 256 reloads, the loops and the event logic and 60 for the table walk; the recovery besides its circuit 110: passes, checkpoints, bisection and moves; setup, cut and slice 160).

### 3.5 Register file
Convention H3: the machine has 800 registers of 256 bits. The generated line needs 773 (`allocate` gives the registers, 0 moves; the selftest executes the allocated code on a register file and compares it with the step). The claim assumes R >= 773; 800 is declared and counted as 25,600 bytes of memory.

### 3.6 Generator code and peak storage of the setup
The generator that builds the straight line at the start of the run is part of the machine code. It is the freestanding C program `GEN_C` embedded in `experiments/bsrho.py` (`python3 bsrho.py --gen-c`). It builds the network of Section 3.2 (pivot adders, the constant-bit carry, the three-addend columns with their dry trials) and allocates the registers in two passes with no node table. Pass 0 builds the network and counts the uses of every node (8 bits per node). Pass 1 builds it again; every signal carries its register, an operand whose use count reaches zero frees its register (registers 256 and above; output plane j is pinned to register j), the result takes the lowest free register or a new one, and the 5-byte record is written. A dry trial sets a flag under which a gate is counted and given a fresh node number but nothing is recorded, and the node counter is restored afterwards, as `Gen.add3` removes its trial nodes. This is possible because the network has no shared and no unused gate (Section 3.2) and its output gates are the last 256 gates in order (the generator checks the second, the selftest the first). The code of the generator is charged at its size compiled to WebAssembly by clang -Os: 2,149 instructions (`llvm-objdump -d`, one line per instruction), 36 bytes each = 77,364 bytes.
Its scratch (`gen_scratch`): use counts of 16,803 nodes, 16,804 bytes; signal state 2,400 (16 x 32 state words 2,048, two 32-word temporaries 256, the free-register bitmap 96); constant tables 256 (IV, K, permutation, G-call indices, the four synthesis columns); shadow stack 2,048; result record 68: 21,576 bytes.
Layout. The setup phase holds the main code (at its final size, 134,062 bytes; it is written as the generator emits it), the advice K (32), the counters and plane copy (16,384), the generator's code image (77,364) and its scratch (21,576): 249,418 bytes. The generator is never called after setup. In the search phase the same region holds the table (872,438 bytes) and the register file (25,600): main code + advice + counters + register file + table = 1,048,516 bytes. The memory of the entry is the larger of the two, 1,048,516 bytes.
Measured run of the generator (not the machine model, which is the byte account above): `GEN_C` compiled to WebAssembly (clang -Os, 2,048-byte shadow stack) and run in a sandbox whose guest linear memory is capped at 16 pages = 1,048,576 bytes emits the identical program: 16546 instructions, 773 registers, 16,803 nodes, 491,076 primitive calls (both passes), the same q, and the instruction text equal line by line to the Python generator's (sha256 eba707bf9a0322b7295d2b0adcff8790697f796cd51085884c42a09e79e7eeb7 for both). Its static arrays total 171,766 bytes; the guest uses 3 pages. It executes 30,441,703 wasm instructions, below the 101,524,400 word operations the ledger charges for the generator.
No other run determines a number of the claim: the circuit, its size, its registers, q and the instruction count come from the generator; the plan (Tp, B, c, c_r, tmax) is a deterministic computation of `plan(256)` from the stated formulas; the experiments and checks of Sections 1, 5 and 6 test the program and select nothing.

### 3.7 Preprocessing and the prefix
The prefix K is the result of a fixed procedure (`python3 bsrho.py --choose-key`, function `choose_key`): coordinate descent from the zero prefix over the words m0, ..., m7 in this order, each over the 16 values v * 2^28 (v = 0, ..., 15) in increasing order; a value is kept when the number of gates of the network (`trace(256, key)`, unused gates dropped) is smaller than the best so far; the sweeps repeat until one keeps nothing. It made 241 evaluations (two sweeps) and returned K = (0x80000000, 0, 0x80000000, 0, 0x70000000, 0, 0, 0) with 16546 gates (the zero prefix: 16572). Each evaluation is charged as one pass of the generator, 200 operations per primitive call and per node; the sum over the 241 evaluations is 12,658,758,600 operations, a row of N (3.4). K is a constant of the program and of the generator (`KEY`), held in 32 bytes of memory (Section 7), and it is declared as nonuniform advice of 32 bytes (nonuniform_advice_log2_bytes = 5). The claim declares preprocessing_log2 = 24.26: log2((102,338,000 + 12,658,758,600) / 638) = 24.2536, the setup and generator row and the choice of K, both inside time_log2. The memory figure covers the preprocessing: the generator runs in the setup phase (249,418 bytes); the choice of K runs before the attempt and leaves only K.

### 3.8 Memory and the scalar score
The scoring policy is `hashsmash-scalar-tm-v1`: score = time_log2 + max(0, memory_log2_bytes - 20). The program, its registers, counters, advice and table are 1,048,516 bytes (Section 7), not above 2^20 = 1,048,576; the claim states memory_log2_bytes = 20, and the term is 0: the scalar score is 124.69481.
