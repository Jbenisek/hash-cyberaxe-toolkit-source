# blake3-r3: plain birthday with 256 bit-sliced lanes, three-addend pair-carry columns, a 32-byte prefix, a quotient-filter table and checkpoint recovery (B3R3s)

Credited derivative of mitchuski's 10ce9f59-80a1-4304-92cc-ca4dede3bbcc (blake3 round 3, time_log2 124.70692: our b9f7a2bc program at three rounds with a three-addend pair-carry column for every all-variable a + b + m) and of our own 3c2d1220-540f-480a-81ca-b26e5ae2173a (blake3 round 3, 124.76596: this program family at three rounds, its proof and experiments). From the note of 10ce9f59 we took the column (the carry pair (u, U) = U ? 1 : 2u, its templates 0 and 1, the choice of addend order and template by a dry trial, the boundary columns) and re-implemented it in our generator and its C port; we did not use or run any file of 10ce9f59. The pair encoding is the modified double full adder of Demenkov, Kojevnikov, Kulikov and Yaroslavtsev (2010), as 10ce9f59 states. The rest of the program is ours (3c2d1220 and its lineage, Section 8), with the pivot full adder and the mask walk g = f XOR q of jungjipdo's 6ae36351-5836-4a46-ad34-227688264123, re-implemented from its proof text.
The target is the plain one-block form: the message is 64 bytes, a fixed 32-byte prefix K || x, flags 11, standard IV, unkeyed, the complete digest. K is declared nonuniform advice (32 bytes, counted as memory; its offline choice is charged in the time bound, Section 3.7).

What changed against 3c2d1220 (124.76596), each item in the program and counted in the ledger:
1. The step: 16,546 gates instead of 17,238. Every a + b + m with 96 variable planes is one ripple of carry pairs (Section 3.2; the column of 10ce9f59, plus four exact-synthesis columns for stored-polarity contexts in which both templates need 10 gates, and a 2-gate carry for a constant-1 full adder with opposite stored polarities). The prefix K replaces 0^32: the words m0..m7 are constants of the circuit, chosen by a stated procedure (Section 3.7).
2. The stage-1 test ORs the first 13 low planes and compares them with all ones before the other 19 (Section 3.3): 14 operations per batch, 21 more on a passing batch, with a halt on the number of passing batches.
3. The table is a quotient filter of 7-byte slots (Section 7): a 52-bit key of which 16 bits are implied by the slot position, 36 stored, a 17-bit trail number and 3 filter bits. It holds 124,633 entries (3c2d1220: 87,359 entries of 10 bytes).
4. The recovery stores checkpoints (Section 2, step 6): pass 1 recomputes both lengths, at most c_r = ceil(12.4 L) batches (a halt beyond; its loss P_rec is in Lemma 2), and keeps every D-th point of each trail in the table region; pass 2 is a bisection over the checkpoints and one short walk, at most 17 D batches. The age cap is c = 20 L.
The plan follows: Tp = 1913, B, c and the table are re-planned. time_log2 124.69481 against 124.76596 (-0.07115) and against 10ce9f59's 124.70692 (-0.01211). No new premise: H1 and H3 are those of 3c2d1220.

## 1. Target and relation

Target `blake3-r3-prefix-v1` (the benchmark's blake3 profile at 3 rounds). The two colliding messages are 64 bytes, M(x) = K || LE32(x): the 32 bytes K (words m0..m7 = 0x80000000, 0, 0x80000000, 0, 0x70000000, 0, 0, 0, little endian; hex 0000008000000000000000800000000000000070000000000000000000000000) followed by the 256-bit integer x (words m8..m15, little endian). It is one chunk of one block: chaining value IV, counter 0, block length 64, flags 11 = CHUNK_START | CHUNK_END | ROOT, 3 rounds with the BLAKE3 message permutation between rounds, digest = the 8 words v[i] XOR v[i+8] (i < 8), little endian (this is `verifier/blake3.py:blake3(M(x), 3)`). Write f(x) for the digest read as a 256-bit little-endian integer.
The walk is on g(x) = f(x) XOR q, where q is the fixed 256-bit mask (weight 124) of the polarities of the 256 digest planes of the circuit that the generator of Section 3.2 builds; q is printed by `python3 bsrho.py --ledger`. g(x) = g(y) if and only if f(x) = f(y): XOR with a constant is a bijection. The attack outputs x != y with g(x) = g(y), hence f(x) = f(y), and M(x) != M(y) are two distinct 64-byte messages with equal complete 256-bit digests (a 64-byte string is in the profile's message domain).
The program was compared outside the shipped experiments (`ext/ref_check.py`, not shipped) with the organizer reference `verifier/blake3.py:blake3(K || x, 3)`: the scalar digest on 2,048 random x, the bit-sliced step (stored planes XOR q) on 2,048 lanes, the packed recovery circuit on 448 values: 0 differences in each. The program's selftest (`python3 bsrho.py --selftest`) compares the step with the scalar compression on 4,096 lanes and on 4,096 lanes chained for 7 steps, executes the register-allocated code on a register file, checks the width-18 chain, the packed circuit, the table against a dictionary and the recovery with two checkpoint spacings, and prints the numbers of shared and unused gates of the generator (both 0).

## 2. The attack (program `experiments/bsrho.py`, sections 3 to 6)

Parameters for n = 256: N = 2^256, 256 lanes. A point x is distinguished (a DP) when its low 32 bits are zero and the 90-bit number F = (x >> 32) mod 2^90 is below Tp = 1913. The density is theta = 1913 / 2^122 and L = 1 / theta = 2^111.0984. The key of a DP is the 41 bits of x above bit 121 followed by the 11 low bits of F (F < 1913 <= 2^11, so those 11 bits are F): 52 bits.
One fresh uniform 256-bit word r (one operation). Trail t (t = 0, 1, 2, ...) starts at s_t = r + t mod 2^256 and follows x -> g(x) until a DP.
1. 256 trails run in the 256 lanes: lane l of the bit planes holds the current point of its trail. A batch is one evaluation of g for all 256 lanes by the generated straight line of Section 3.2. Batch counter b = 0, 1, ...; the start of a trail is the point of its lane before the batch in which it was inserted.
2. After every batch the stage-1 test (Section 3.3) ORs the 13 lowest planes. If every lane has a 1 among them (the OR is all ones) the batch ends. Otherwise (a passing batch) it ORs the other 19 of the 32 low planes; the lanes with all 32 low bits zero are the stage-1 hits.
3. For each hit the lane is extracted and the window test F < Tp decides whether it is a DP. A lane at a DP ends its trail. If its key is in the table, go to 6. Otherwise store t under the key (Section 7) and start trail t' = next index in that lane (insert the 256 bits of r + t' into the lane).
4. At every multiple of S = 2^48 batches: a lane whose trail has age >= c = 20 L batches is restarted with a new index (a cut trail).
5. The search halts (failure) after B batches, after pmax passing batches, after e2max stage-1 hits, when the table holds tmax = 124,633 keys, when a second trail would be cut, or if step 6 halts or finds no pair.
6. Recovery of the two trails A, B with the same key. Pass 1: both walk together from their starts r + t, r + t' (two lanes of one packed circuit, one step per batch); after each step a lane that has not finished stores its point as a checkpoint when the step number is a multiple of D = ceil(c_r / 8192) and is tested for being a DP; the step at which a lane first is a DP is its length. More than c_r = ceil(12.4 L) steps halts the run. Each trail has at most 8,192 checkpoints, kept in the table region (the table is not needed any more: the run ends with this recovery). Pass 2: let A be the longer trail, d = l1 - l2 >= 0 and A_j, B_j the points after j steps. P(j): A_(j+d) = B_j is false and then true (a merge persists). If the two DP points differ (a key match without a merge) or P(0) holds (the start of B lies on A), there is no pair. Otherwise a bisection over the checkpoint indices i keeps P(lo D) false and P(min(hi D, l2)) true, starting from lo = 0, hi = floor(l2 / D) + 1; a probe of index i loads checkpoint i + floor(d / D) of A, walks it d mod D steps (one lane) and compares with checkpoint i of B. At the end the two lanes walk together from A_(lo D + d), B_(lo D) for at most D steps; at the first step with g(a) = g(b) and a != b the pair (a, b) is output. The packed circuit (609 operations: the compression and one XOR of each of the 8 output words with the 32-bit word of q) evaluates both lanes.
Constants (computed by `plan(256)` of the program from the stated formulas, printed by `python3 bsrho.py --ledger`): Tp = 1913, L = 2^122 / 1913, B = 1324599156626000000000000000000000000, Q = 256 B = 0.99652 * 2^128 evaluations, e2max = 2 B 256 / 2^32 + 64 = 157904524400949478149414062564, pmax = ceil(2 B P13) + 64 = 81512161205425838555432838024477137 with P13 = 1 - (1 - 2^-13)^256 = 0.0307686 (the probability that 256 uniform lanes pass the first 13 planes), c = ceil(20 L) = 55587161350127166666128889086475466 (2^115.4203), c_r = ceil(12.4 L) = 34464040037078843332999911233614789 (2^114.7307), D = 4207036137338725992797840726760, table capacity tmax = 124,633 keys in 124,634 slots of 7 bytes. The trail numbers t stay below 2^17 (256 starts, at most 124,633 finished trails, at most 1 cut trail: t <= 256 + 124,633 + 1 < 2^17), so the 17-bit field never overflows (the program asserts LANES + tmax + CMAX < 2^17 in its plan and t < 2^17 when it stores an entry); no length is stored.
Per attempt: one fresh r; no restart, no amplification, no stored collision. The mask q is derived by the generator at setup and is written into the recovery circuit as immediates. The only advice is K.
