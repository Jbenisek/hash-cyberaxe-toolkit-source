## 5. Premise H1 and its evidence

H1 is the model premise of Section 4. It is a premise about one fixed function g and about one attempt; it cannot be tested at width 256: the full 256-bit functional graph, trails longer than 2^40 and 2^128 evaluations cannot be observed. It is declared and not proved: an assumption, no proof is known. Its content, in the terms Lemma 2 uses, is four clauses: (a) for the inputs the search evaluates, the outputs behave as the outputs of F, uniform and independent, so that two distinct evaluated inputs collide with probability 2^-256 (this entry uses no input difference and does not claim that none exists); (b) the iterates of g from the starts r + t have the first-collision law of a random mapping; (c) whether an iterate is a DP is independent of the collision events and has probability 1913 / 2^122; (d) adaptivity: the program chooses its points, and which trails finish, are cut or abort, from earlier outputs of g; clause (d) is that this adaptivity does not lower the collision hazard, that is, for every fresh evaluation, given the whole history of the run and which later outputs are distinguished points, the output equals a given earlier non-distinguished output with probability at least 2^-256 (the lazy-sampling hazard that F has by construction, L1 of Section 4). Lemma 2 proves the bound for F from the hazard alone (the first collision of the run and the detection delay, no conditioning on which trails finish), and (d) is the premise that g has that hazard. The prefix K and the mask q fix the function g; H1 is stated for this g. Nothing else in the success bound is heuristic: Lemma 1, the lazy-sampling facts, the first-collision bound, the Chernoff bounds, the budgets and the cut count are proved in Section 4 for F; Sections 3 and 7 are counts of the program. Sensitivity: a hazard (1 - eps) / N costs the same as a deficit eps in clause (a): 0.0073 bit for eps = 0.01, 0.0370 bit for eps = 0.05, 0.0760 bit for eps = 0.10, 0.2075 bit for eps = 0.25 (Section 4).
The two organizer experiments (Section 6) check that the program is right on the real target: the organizer recomputes every returned pair. They are program checks, reproducible observations consistent with the premise; no number of this entry is estimated from their counts, no interval is formed, and the entry does not rely on the independence of trials.
At width 18 a halt "recovery found no pair" (a trail start lying on another trail) is frequent; at width 256 it has probability below 2^-100 (Section 4). It happens equally for the ideal function.
Not tested: the full 256-bit functional graph; iterations of length above 2^40; the bit-sliced search above width 18. A deviation of g from H1 that raises the collision rate only helps; one that lowers it by eps costs the extra time above.

## 6. Experiments

Two organizer experiments run the shipped program `experiments/bsrho.py` (it dispatches on the experiment id; the standard library only).
`bsrho-n18`: per trial, from the trial seed, one attempt of the 256-lane search at width n = 18 (DP = low 2 bits zero, plan(18): 8 batches, the stage-1 test, the quotient-filter table, the cut rule, the recovery with checkpoints on the packed circuit) on the target chain g (the block K || x, the low 18 bits of g); the trial returns the two 64-byte messages K || x, K || y when the attempt succeeded and two nulls otherwise. The checked event is that the complete 3-round digests agree in the low 18 bits and the messages are distinct. Observations (untrusted): the same attempt on an ideal function, and one family-law run with a fresh key at n = 12.
`proj-k0-w256`: per trial, 2048 points of the target at width 256 evaluated with the bit-sliced circuit (source by trial index mod 3: uniform inputs, counter inputs r + i, iterates of g), returning two points whose digests agree in the low 21 bits when the set contains such a pair. The digests are recovered from the stored planes by XOR with q. The checked event is that the digests agree in the low 21 bits and the messages are distinct. Observations (untrusted): the source and the number of equal pairs in the low 21 bits.
They check the circuit at full width, the bit-sliced search, lane insertion and extraction, the table, the recovery, the mask q and the message construction with the prefix K on the real target. They do not measure the cost or the success probability at width 256. Running times on the development machine for 256 trials: 3.5 s and 5.1 s.

## 7. Memory

Table: 124,634 slots * 7 bytes = 872,438 bytes (the table region is empty until the search starts). It is a quotient filter (Bender et al., VLDB 2012; the stored key bits implied by the slot as in eprint 2011/387 Section 2 and 2017/581): S = tmax + 1 = 124,634 slots, l = floor(log2 S) = 16. The 52-bit key is split into the quotient (its top 16 bits), which is the home slot, and the remainder (36 bits). A slot holds 56 bits: the remainder, the 17-bit trail number t and three bits (occupied: some stored key has this home slot; continuation: the entry continues the run of the slot before; shifted: the entry is not in its home slot). The entries of one quotient form a run; runs lie in quotient order, shifted right cyclically past used slots; a lookup walks back to the start of the cluster and forward one run per occupied home slot to the key's run and scans it, an insertion places the new entry at the end of its run and moves the following entries up to the first empty slot one slot right (class `QF` of the program, `find` and `put`; the selftest compares it with a dictionary). At most tmax = 124,633 keys are stored (a halt on a full table), so a slot is always empty and every walk ends. No length is stored (a length is at most c_r and is recomputed by pass 1 of the recovery).
Code: 17,186 gate words * 5 bytes (the step 16546, the 31 ORs of stage 1, the recovery circuit 609) + 1,337 other instructions * 36 bytes (loop control 3, the stage-1 compares and branches 4, handlers 1,060, recovery besides its circuit 110, setup, cut and slice 160) = 85,930 + 48,132 = 134,062 bytes.
Counters and copies: 16,384 bytes (trail numbers, birth batches, counters, and the 8,192-byte copy of the 256 planes used by the handlers). The 800 registers: 25,600 bytes. Advice K: 32 bytes. Memory peak = max(setup phase 249,418, search phase 1,048,516) = 1,048,516 bytes = 2^19.99992, 60 bytes under 2^20; the preprocessing is inside the setup phase. Claim `memory_log2_bytes` = 20 (the log2 of the 2^20-byte budget); the scalar policy adds 0 (Section 3.8). `python3 bsrho.py --ledger` prints the total. During the recovery the checkpoints (at most 2 * 8,192 points of 32 bytes = 524,288 bytes) are written into the table region, which the run no longer needs (the program asserts 8,192 * 64 <= 7 (tmax + 1)).
The table size is the largest that fits: tmax = floor((1,048,576 - 64 - 134,062 - 25,600 - 16,384 - 32) / 7) = 124,633, and the extra slot comes out of the 64 bytes. Tp = 1913 is the largest integer for which the success bound of Section 4 reaches 0.39 at the largest mean number of trails that the table holds with capacity tail below 2^-40.
