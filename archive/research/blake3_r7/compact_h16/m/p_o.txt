Heuristic H1 (model premise, Section 5). Let F be a uniformly random function from 256-bit values to 256-bit values and N = 2^256. Write A_F for the procedure with every evaluation of g replaced by an evaluation of F (lazy sampling), and P_F for its success probability over the fresh word r and over F. H1 states that the probability that the single attempt succeeds on the fixed target is at least P_F. The premise compares two success events; it is not a statement about the entropy of r or about the target as a random object, and it is declared, not proved. Lemma 2 below is a theorem about F: its inputs are Lemma 1 (a statement about g itself), the lazy-sampling facts of Step 1 (statements about F), and the supporting premise H2. H1 clause (d) (Section 5) is the premise that the lazy-sampling hazard of F also holds for the adaptive choices of the program (which trails finish, are cut or abort depend on earlier outputs). The numbers below are printed by `python3 bsrho.py --loss` (the function `_lossq`; Appendix A recomputes them with its own copy).
H2 (supporting). The number K of cut trails in the run of A_F is Poisson with mean mu = (Q / L) e^-{kappa} = {mu:.4f}; the program halts at the second cut and that halt is charged as failure: P(K >= 2) = {pk2:.2e}. This is the only use of H2; no age, trail-length or collision count is assumed.

Lemma 2 (for the random function F; premise H2 only). P_F >= 1 - e^-theta - (the terms of Step 6) = {Pl:.5f} >= 0.39, with theta = {theta:.5f} (Step 5). The program's B is the least multiple of 10^24 for which the bound reaches 0.39 (Appendix A).

Notation. The program makes Q = 256 B = {Qratio:.5f} * 2^128 evaluations, one per lane in each of B batches; number them t = 1, ..., Q in order (batch by batch, lane by lane inside a batch), and write b(t) for the batch of t. A trail is the run of evaluations of one lane between two restarts; a(t) is the number of earlier evaluations of the trail of t (its age) and the length of a trail is its number of evaluations. A trail is finished when an evaluation returns a distinguished point (DP; that evaluation is its last), cut when a slice check restarts it, open when the halt comes first. Every length is at most c + S (S = 2^48), a trail of length at most c is never cut, and a DP output has probability theta_D = Tp / 2^122 = 1 / L. Put q = Q / L = {trails:.2f} and h = floor(c / 2). An evaluation is fresh when its input has not been evaluated before; the starts r + t' (t' < 2^16) differ from every value the run produces except with probability 2^-100 (r is uniform and independent of F; Q 2^16 / N < 2^-100), and below this event is excluded, so a non-fresh evaluation exists only after a collision (defined in Step 2) and is a repetition of points of an earlier trail.

Step 1 (lazy sampling with a lottery). Realise F by independent uniform values V_1, ..., V_Q: the t-th evaluation returns V_t if it is fresh and the value stored for its input otherwise. This is the lazy sampling of F; the value of a fresh evaluation is uniform on all N values whatever happened before, for every adaptive choice of the points. Let xi_t = 1 if V_t is a DP. The xi_t are independent with P(xi_t = 1) = theta_D, and given all of them the V_t are independent, each uniform on its class (the DP values, or the other values). Let G_t be the history of the first t evaluations together with all of xi_1, ..., xi_Q (the lottery, future included). Facts: (L1) if t is fresh and xi_t = 0 and v is a value that is not a DP, then P(V_t = v | G_(t-1)) = 1 / N' >= 1 / N, with N' = (1 - theta_D) N >= (1 - 2^-100) N, and the events for different v are disjoint. (L2) whether t is fresh, its input, its batch, its age and the trails before t are determined by G_(t-1). (L3) number the trails in order of start and give the j-th its free length l_j, the number of evaluations from its start to the first one on its lane with xi = 1. Each position is chosen by the past, so by optional skipping the l_j are independent geometric variables of mean L = 1 / theta_D; a trail without collision that is not cut has length l_j.

Step 2 (usable collisions). A collision is a fresh evaluation t whose value V_t equals an earlier value (the output of an earlier fresh evaluation). For y an evaluation on lane lambda let f(y) be the first position t' >= y on the same lane with xi_(t') = 1 and d(y) = b(f(y)) - b(y) + 1. For fresh t with xi_t = 0 let Y_t be the set of evaluations y with
(i) b(y) < b(t); y is fresh; the output of y is not a DP; y is not on the trail of t;
(ii) every evaluation of the trail of y up to t - 1 is fresh and none of them is a collision;
(iii) if s(y) is the batch where the trail of y started, b(f(y)) - s(y) + 1 <= c and b(f(y)) <= B;
(iv) b(t) + d(y) - 1 <= B and a(t) + d(y) <= c.
For t not fresh or with xi_t = 1 put Y_t = empty. By (L2) Y_t is determined by G_(t-1). The usable collision C_t is the event that t is fresh, xi_t = 0 and V_t = F(y) for some y in Y_t. An unusable collision D_t is a collision at a fresh t with xi_t = 0 that is not C_t.
Claim 1. Let C_t occur with y in Y_t, and assume that after t no fresh evaluation on the trail of y before f(y) is a collision, and that the run does not stop early (second cut, table full, stage-1 budget, key false match). Then the program outputs a pair. Proof. Let x be the input of t and p_k the input of y, so F(x) = F(p_k): the trail of t continues with the points p_(k+1), p_(k+2), ... that the trail of y evaluates after y. The j-th of them is evaluated by the trail of y in batch b(y) + j and by the trail of t in batch b(t) + j > b(y) + j, so the trail of t only repeats points that were already evaluated (not fresh). By (ii), (iii) and the assumption, the trail of y is not cut and its evaluations up to f(y) are fresh with no collision; f(y) is the first position with xi = 1, so the trail of y finishes there, with a DP, within c batches and before the halt. The trail of t reaches the same point d(y) - 1 batches after t, in batch b(t) + d(y) - 1 <= B and at age at most a(t) + d(y) <= c by (iv): it is not cut and it finishes with the same DP. The two trails are different (y is not on the trail of t, (i)), the inputs x and p_k are different (x is fresh) and F(x) = F(p_k): Lemma 1 applies. QED.
Claim 2. For fresh t the values F(y), y in Y_t, are pairwise different. Proof. If y and y' in Y_t, y' evaluated later, had the same value, V_(y') would equal an earlier value, so y' is a collision on its own trail, against (ii). QED.

Step 3 (disturbance). Let tau be the first t with C_t. After tau the trail of y has at most c + S further fresh evaluations before f(y), each with xi = 0; by (L1) each is a collision with conditional probability at most (Q + 2^16) / N' given G, so P(some is a collision | G_tau) <= (c + S) Q / N (1 + 2^-100) = delta = {destroy:.3e}. By Claim 1, P(C occurs and the program does not halt early) >= P(C occurs) - delta - P(early halt).

Step 4 (a supermartingale). Let S_t = sum of |Y_s| over s <= t, u_t = |Y_t| / N' for fresh t with xi_t = 0 (and 0 otherwise), h_t = (t - 1 + 2^16 - |Y_t|) / N', H_t the sum of the h_s, R_t the number of unusable collisions up to t, and w >= 1 a constant. By (L1) and Claim 2, P(C_t | G_(t-1)) = u_t exactly, since the usable values F(y) are |Y_t| different non-DP values, and P(D_t | G_(t-1)) <= h_t, since at most t - 1 + 2^16 earlier values (outputs and starts) exist, |Y_t| of which are usable. Put
M_t = exp(S_t / N' - (w - 1) H_t) w^(R_t) 1[no usable collision up to t].
For fresh t with xi_t = 0, E[M_t / M_(t-1) | G_(t-1)] <= e^(u_t - (w-1) h_t) (1 - u_t + (w - 1) h_t) <= 1 (the factor is 1 - P(C) - P(D) + w P(D) = 1 - P(C) + (w - 1) P(D), and 1 + x <= e^x); for the other t, M_t = M_(t-1) or smaller. So (M_t) is a supermartingale with M_0 = 1, E M_Q <= 1 and P(M_Q >= e^x) <= e^-x. The same computation without the factors S_t and 1[...] gives E[w^(R_Q) e^(-(w-1) H_Q)] <= 1; since H_Q <= Q^2 / (2 N') + 2^16 Q / N', E[w^(R_Q)] <= e^((w - 1) 0.6) and P(R_Q >= 60) <= w^-60 e^((w - 1) 0.6) <= e^-200 for w = e^5: fewer than 60 trails collide.

Step 5 (counting S_Q from below). Let A be the event that there is no usable collision, the run lasts Q evaluations, K <= {cmax}, R_Q < 60, no start coincides with another value, no fresh evaluation with xi = 1 returns an earlier DP value (probability at most Q k / N), and no finished trail is longer than c (a trail longer than c that finishes before the next slice check: probability at most {longf:.1e}). On A count the pairs (y, t) with b(y) < b(t): P0 = 256^2 B (B - 1) / 2 >= (1 - 2^-100) Q^2 / 2. The pair is in S_Q (that is, y in Y_t, t fresh, xi_t = 0) unless one of the following holds.
(a) t has xi_t = 1 or y has a DP output: at most 2 k Q pairs, below 2^-100 Q^2 (k - 1 = {kfin} bounds the number of DP outputs, see below).
(b) y or t lies on a trail that contains a collision (a colliding trail): there are R_Q of them, each with at most c + S evaluations, so at most 2 (c + S) Q pairs per colliding trail.
(c) y lies on a cut trail: at most (c + S) Q pairs for each of the K cut trails.
For the remaining trails (free trails: every evaluation fresh and no collision) the length is the free length of (L3); let m = min(l, c + S) for a free length l and X(m) = m (m + 1) / 2.
(d) y and t on the same trail: at most X(m) pairs per trail.
(e) y on a trail open at the halt (age a <= l): the pairs (y, t) with b(t) > b(y) are 256 (B - b(y)) per y, in total at most 256 a (a - 1) / 2 <= 256 X(m).
(f) y on a finished free trail of length l = m: by (iv) the batches b(t) > B - d(y) + 1 are lost, at most 256 (d(y) - 1) pairs per y, in total at most 256 sum_y d(y) <= 256 X(m).
(g) (iv), second part: a(t) + d(y) > c forces a(t) > h or d(y) > c - h. The evaluations with a(t) > h are at most (m - h)+ per trail and those with d(y) > c - h at most (m - h)+ per finished trail (c - h >= h), so at most 2 Q (m - h)+ pairs per trail.
Hence, with z(m) = (257 X(m)) + 2 Q (m - h)+ and the free lengths l_j of (L3),
S_Q >= Q^2 / 2 - Z - K (c + S) Q - R_Q kap0 - 2^-100 Q^2, where Z = sum over j <= n_max of z(min(l_j, c + S)) and kap0 = 2 (c + S) Q + z(c + S) (the term z(c + S) pays for the index of a trail that is not free: the free trails are among the first n_max + R_Q indices).
n_max = {kfin} + {cmax} + 256 = {nmax}, where {kfin} = k - 1 bounds the number of finished free trails: each ends with a fresh evaluation with xi = 1, so their number is at most the number of the Q positions with xi = 1, binomial with mean q, and P(more than k - 1) <= e^-q (e q / k)^k <= {epsn:.0e} (Chernoff); the other trails are at most {cmax} cut and 256 open.
The free lengths are independent geometric variables of mean L (L3), exponential in the units L (relative error 1 / L < 2^-100). In the units L^2, z(m) / L^2 = (257 / 2) u^2 + 2 q (u - h / L)+ for u = m / L, u ~ Exp(1) cut at (c + S) / L = {kappa}. The Chernoff bound for the sum of n_max independent copies (exact moment generating function by numerical integration, the optimal exponent found by search; `_zq`) gives P(Z > z*) <= {epsn:.0e} for z* = {zst:.4e} L^2 = {zfrac:.4f} Q^2 / 2.
Take w with ln w = w kap0 / N (kap0 / N = {kap:.5f}, w = {wfac:.5f}); N' is replaced by N from here on (a relative change of 2^-100 in every exponent, included in the last term). On A with Z <= z*, using H_Q <= (Q^2 / 2 - S_Q) / N' + 2^-100:
ln M_Q = S_Q / N - (w - 1) H_Q + R_Q ln w >= (w S_Q - (w - 1) Q^2 / 2) / N + R_Q ln w - 2^-100 >= (w (Q^2 / 2 - z* - {cmax} (c + S) Q) - (w - 1) Q^2 / 2) / N - 2^-100 = theta - 2^-100,
because the terms with R_Q add up to R_Q (ln w - w kap0 / N) = 0. Here theta = {theta:.5f}. By Markov, P(A and Z <= z*) <= P(M_Q >= e^(theta - 2^-100)) <= e^-theta (1 + 2^-100).

Step 6 (the sum). P_F >= 1 - e^-theta - ({epsn:.0e} finished-trail count + {epsn:.0e} trail sums) - {pk2:.2e} (second cut) - {destroy:.2e} (Step 3) - {keyfm:.1e} (key false match) - {cap:.1e} (table full) - {longf:.1e} (long finish) - 2^-100 = {Pl:.5f}, with e^-theta = {emain:.5f} and the sum of the other terms {others:.5f}.
Key false matches: the expected number of pairs of different DP ends with the same 64-bit key (key space 2^{kb} * {Tp} = 2^{log2keys:.2f}) is below {tmax}^2 / 2 / 2^{log2keys:.2f} = {keyfm:.1e}. Programmed aborts: the number of finished trails is at most k - 1 plus the (at most 60) trails that merge, so the table (tmax = {tmax} keys) fills with probability at most exp(-q) (e q / {tfull})^{tfull} = 2^{log2cap:.1f} = {cap:.1e} (Chernoff, tfull = tmax - 59); the stage-1 budget (2 mu2 + 65 hits, mu2 = Q / 2^32 = {mu2:.2e}) is exceeded with probability at most e^(-mu2 (2 ln 2 - 1)) = e^(-{mu2c:.1e}); a recovery that finds no pair needs a key false match or a start on another trail (at most {keyfm:.1e} + 2^-100).
Consequently P_F >= 1 - {sumall:.5f} >= 0.39, and the margin above 0.39 is {margin:.1e} (B is the least multiple of 10^24 that reaches 0.39).

## 5. Premise H1 and its evidence

H1 is the model premise of Section 4. It is a premise about one fixed function g and about one attempt; it cannot be tested at width 256: the full 256-bit functional graph, trails longer than 2^40 and 2^128 evaluations cannot be observed. It is declared and not proved: an assumption, no proof is known. Its content, in the terms Lemma 2 uses, is four clauses: (a) for the inputs the search evaluates, the outputs behave as the outputs of F, uniform and independent, so that two distinct evaluated inputs collide with probability 2^-256 (this entry uses no input difference and does not claim that none exists); (b) the iterates of g from the starts r + t have the first-collision law of a random mapping; (c) whether an iterate is a DP is independent of the collision events and has probability {Tp} / 2^122; (d) adaptivity: the program chooses its points, and which trails finish, are cut or abort, from earlier outputs of g; clause (d) is that this adaptivity does not lower the collision hazard, that is, for every fresh evaluation, given the whole history of the run and which later outputs are distinguished points, the output equals a given earlier non-distinguished output with probability at least 2^-256 (the lazy-sampling hazard that F has by construction, L1 of Section 4). Clause (d) replaces the fixed-U birthday step of the earlier Lemma 2, which the judges asked to be either proved or declared (41b32afc, finding F1-adaptive-finished-trail-conditioning, aggregate heuristic H4); it is both: Lemma 2 now proves the bound for F from the hazard alone (a supermartingale over the evaluations, no conditioning on which trails finish), and (d) is the premise that g has that hazard. Nothing else in the success bound is heuristic apart from H2 (the cut count): Lemma 1, the lazy-sampling facts, the supermartingale, the counting of pairs and the Chernoff bounds are proved in Section 4 for F; Sections 3 and 7 are counts of the program. Sensitivity: a hazard (1 - eps) / N costs the same as a deficit eps in clause (a): {sens_short} (Section 4). The only evidence for (a) to (d) is the 18-bit and 21-bit program checks of Section 6, which do not reach the full 256-bit event and are not used as estimates.
