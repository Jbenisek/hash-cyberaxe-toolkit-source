How to play

  1. Pick the difference. Toggle the message words (W0–W15) that carry a difference. That set is your shape.
  2. Climb. Each row is one more step of SHA-256. A row passes when a nonzero difference can still keep every expanded word you left off at zero. CLIMB runs up until a row fails; STEP goes one row at a time.
  3. Active words. On a row you can let its new word stay active (it tiles on, costs you nothing in this check but marks the route) or leave it off to demand it be zero.
  4. Reach the frontier. Row 40 is where no one has a real path. Reach it — or reach 38/39 a way the team does not already know — and the game saves it to your Finds and offers a line to send it for the real test.

Honest: passing a row is necessary for a real collision path, never proof of one. The real test is the signed characteristic plus a solver — the team's hours-long job. This is reduced-round research math (37–40 of 64 steps), the kind studied openly in papers; a pass is nowhere near a real collision.

Trust: the instrument runs five controls before it lets you climb. The two settled shapes must stop exactly where the research says, and a recorded rank must match. If a control fails, climbing is switched off.
