## Summary

This is a draft for blake3-r2-prefix-v1, lane exploratory. It keeps the pair of lengths (55 and 63 bytes), the six constants and the exact half-collision of `c66f230d`, and changes five things.

1. **Two levels.** The construction is solved in another order (outer step of six words, then X2, then the member). What depends only on the outer step and the member, ten of the lines of a trial, is computed once per outer step into a table of five lists and reused for 2^32 values of X2. A batch then computes only D0 and C2 before its first test.
2. **A sub-class.** Y4 runs over {{members}} of the 524,288 members of the class: those whose e1 = Y3 + Y4 has bits 21 and 26 zero. Under the model the rate of this quarter is {{r_sub}} times the uniform rate, against {{r_class}} for the whole class.
3. **A rule with eight conditions.** Rule A now has eight parity conditions on h1; inside the sub-class it keeps {{kept_exact}} of the {{r_sub}} counted. Three of its right sides depend on the member, so the test reads one word of a second fixed list. Only one batch in {{share_inv}} may run stage 2.
4. **Registers.** Nine words (six masks and three constants of the rule test) stay in registers during a loop over the members. This is a change of the counting convention against `c66f230d`, and it is stated as such.
5. **Clusters.** A *representative* is a trial whose bits 14, 30 and 31 of e1 and of X2 are zero; its {{neighbours}} *neighbours* are the trials reached by flipping any non-empty subset of those six bits, and together they form a *cluster* of {{cluster_size}} trials. The clusters partition the trials of the sub-class (Lemma C). Stage A runs on the representatives, and on the neighbours of a representative only when that representative passes the *gate* {{gate}}, which is four of the eight conditions of rule A. Those four conditions are carried from a trial to its neighbours and the other four are not, so a representative yields {{yield}} further trial-equivalents of trials that satisfy rule A, measured on real messages three times independently (an opened cluster yields about 2.50 such neighbours, and the gate opens for one representative in sixteen). The charge is therefore per trial-equivalent, {{charge}} for {{cluster_equivalents}} of them per representative, in place of {{nocluster_charge}} per trial and a claim of {{nocluster_claim}} without the clusters. This is the part of the package with the least evidence behind it and the most gain in it; what it rests on and what could not be tested are in proof.md Section 10 and below.

**Exact part (no heuristic).** For every choice of eight 32-bit words the construction gives a 55-byte message and a 63-byte message whose complete 2-round digests agree on digest words 0, 2, 5 and 7. One of the eight words is Y4.

**Heuristic part.** 2^{{trials_log2}} trials with Y4 in the sub-class, seven per 256-bit word, in two stages, examined in the clusters of item 5. A trial-equivalent is charged {{charge}} operations (budgeted {{budgeted}}), which is {{cluster_per_rep}} units per representative divided by {{cluster_equivalents}} trial-equivalents; the counted pieces inside it are stage A {{a_ops}} operations and {{a_loads}} loads, stage 2 {{s2_ops}} and {{s2_loads}}, the middle step {{middle_total}} per value of X2, the table {{build_total}} per list word and the outer step {{outer_total}}. H1 assumes that a trial collides and passes rule A with probability at least {{factor}} * 2^-128, and that such trials do not cluster; the count it is set against is {{kept_cons_int}}, {{margin}} times larger. H4 assumes that the yield of a run is the measured mean {{yield}}, and H5 that the trials the algorithm examines carry the average collision mass of the trials rule A keeps and that the collisions of one cluster do not clump. The total is below 2^{{bound_2dec}} and the claim is {{time_log2}}. With the uniform rate the same search would claim {{uniform_claim}}.

**No full 2-round collision is exhibited**, and the search is far beyond feasible computation.

## How the rate is counted

Under the seven-word model the rate is a number of solutions of a fixed system, counted for each value beta of one difference by enumerating the outcomes of one call and counting the other call exactly. For this package the counter works in integers and splits every count by rule A. For the sub-class: {{r_sub_frac}} = {{r_sub}} from 53 values of beta and 317 outcomes; with rule A {{kept_exact_frac}} = {{kept_exact}}; without the 218 outcomes in which some solution violates the rule, {{kept_cons}}, which is the figure H1 is set against. GPT Sol 6.1 found the sub-class and the rule; its fractions and mine are equal (21 fractions, every beta, 3,243 integers on 1,081 outcomes, three per outcome); the figure H1 is set against is mine alone. My recount was not blind. Checks of my counter: complete enumeration with 8-bit words with sub-classes and three-bit parities, 6,805,032 integers, none different.

## What is assumed and what is not shown

- H1, H2 and H3 are assumptions. Both parts of H1, the factor {{factor}} and the absence of clustering of the good trials, are assumed; no experiment measures the factor, and the count it is set against is not a measurement of the trials.
- The model does not hold inside one context for rule A, and in this arrangement not inside one outer step for the call E1; only averages over outer steps have its values (layout runs on a graphics card, for the whole class and the rules of `c66f230d`).
- For the sub-class with the new rule the processor samples are of at most 2^30 real trials (a graphics-card run of 2^44.59 trials came later, below); in my three runs the rule passes with probability 2^-8.00 and {{entry_1}}, {{entry_2}} and {{entry_3}} of the batches run stage 2, against a budget of {{budget_share}}; samples of the review in the ranges of the algorithm give 0.02617 to 0.02620. Single contexts reach {{ctx_max}} and exceed the budget; H3 is a statement about the average of a run.
- A graphics-card run now has the sub-class and the new rule (2^44.59 real trials; rule A passes at 2^-8.000002 and a marker at 2^-32.2 is about 1.18 times richer in the sub-class; its deeper marker at 2^-40.15 has 14 counts against 21.59 expected, which settles nothing at that precision, and the same sub-class on random outer words in a later run has 275 against 288.1), and a scaled-down end-to-end run with real collisions has both the sub-class and an eight-condition rule (121 collisions against 120.23 predicted); neither reaches rule A together with a collision, nor measures the kept mass.
- Scaled-down end-to-end runs now exist for this pair of lengths: in the two-level order 470 collisions against 467.6 predicted in 436 runs (the first 92 runs were low, 56 against 74.8; the later ones were made because of that); in the order of `c66f230d` 345 against 335.76 in a plan fixed beforehand. The larger two-level job (8,448 runs, expectations written down before it ran) is 8,108 against 8,313.6, -2.25 standard deviations: 3.3 percent low with 8-bit words (-2.65), level with 10-bit words (+0.19). The cause is not known; a shortfall of that size is far inside the margin of {{margin}}.
- Real messages in the order of `c66f230d`, whole class: 24 runs of 2^45 trials on another machine, 692 listed E1 outcomes against 690.78; nothing of this kind exists for the sub-class.
- The loops, their ranges and the processing of a passing trial are defined in the text only. The program counts the pieces.
- The sub-class and the rule were chosen by the count, and no measurement on real messages shows the gain of the sub-class.
- H4 and H5, the two heuristics of the clusters, are assumptions of a kind I have not filed before. H4 is set against a measured mean and not a count: the yield {{yield}} was measured three times independently on real messages of this sub-class (40.018 with a standard error of 0.012, 40.032 with 0.075, and 40.027 with 0.010 by Grok 4.7 with a program and a reference of its own; a fourth run gives 40.117 and 39.802). It varies by about 14 per cent between outer tuples, and every run that measured it used random outer words rather than the walk the algorithm makes. The claim is proportional to 1 / (1 + {{yield}}): a yield lower by one part in ten raises it by about 0.15, and a yield of 1 would put it back at {{nocluster_claim}}.
- The gate opens for 0.0625 of the representatives, nine neighbour words then go through stage A (0.5625 per representative) and 0.1364 per representative enter stage 2. These are measured means. The count per value of X2 is not binomial (variance 11 times the binomial one pooled, up to 80 times inside one outer tuple), and the largest share seen for one value of X2 grows with the sample: 1.64 times the mean over 8,192 values, 1.72 over 16,384. Section 8 charges 1.2 times the measured means, a budget of the same kind as the one for the entry to stage 2 and no more proved.
- For the first part of H5 no bias was found in the trials the procedure examines. The collision mass per examined trial, against ordinary trials that satisfy rule A, is 0.99950 plus or minus 0.00036 and 0.99994 plus or minus 0.00006 on the exact law of h1 in two independent measurements, 1.0017 plus or minus 0.0030 on the weighted tally of the 67 listed cells of the first side, and 1.0000 on the four heaviest paths; by direction of the cluster the seven neighbours that flip only bits of X2 are within one error of 1 except on the tally of cells (0.930 plus or minus 0.059). GPT Sol 6.1 computed the same factor exactly rather than by sampling, on three partial averages it could enumerate: 1.008, 0.997 and 1.000. None of this is a proof, the two sides must not be multiplied as independent factors, and all of it stops at proxies well above the collision.
- The second part of H5 cannot be measured. What is measured is the clumping of rule-A passes inside a cluster, at 2^-9 and 2^-19, and it is slightly NEGATIVE (3.0 per cent below independent trials at the four heaviest paths). The collision is at 2^-{{r_sub_prob}}; treating the two levels as a guide to it is an extrapolation of about 100 bits. If an opened cluster fired as one trial, the claim would rise by about 1.3 to 1.8, most of what the clusters gain. Two things would settle it and neither exists: a reduced-word run end to end whose gate is carried to the neighbours at 0.6 to 0.87 as it is at full size, and a proof that each of the six flipped bits still moves the collision after conditioning on a collision at the representative. The two scaled-down runs that do exist ran the procedure with real collisions and found them at the predicted rate (examined neighbours 67 against 68.78, and 113 against 120.72 on fresh trials), but their gates are hardly carried (co-pass 0.26 against 0.25 by chance, where this design has 0.54 to 0.87), so they do not test this. Teams with more computing time or more analysis than I have can run that experiment and look for that proof; that is why both are written out here and in Section 10.
- The neighbour path is not in the submitted program. A helper agent added it to a copy, below the submitted code so the existing self-test is untouched, and counted every piece on the program's own machine, including the result that a neighbour word costs the same as a representative word although its lanes may carry two values of X2. That copy's own self-test puts 50,048 trials in 782 clusters against an independent reference and 7,875 lanes with mixed values of X2 through the packed machine, all right. What no one has counted is one schedule that threads representative word, gate, dispatch, neighbour words and both stages through a single account of the 16 registers, so the charge is a lower bound by whatever that routing costs. The cluster layout also needs about eight times the memory of the arrangement without it, which the declared memory figure covers.
- The second stage is split at the cheap test of Lemma T: 81 units run before it, 12 for the test and 57 only for a word that passes, a split derived from the program's own part table. The test keeps 185,351,809 of the 185,358,111.47 that rule A keeps, exactly, which is 3.7520 times the assumed factor. The 12 units were counted on an earlier machine with a different second stage and were not replayed on this one.
- Three helper agents reviewed the assembled text once; the corrections and the evidence added after their review were not read again by them. The clusters and this evidence were added after that review and have not been reviewed at all. No person has read it.

## Limitations

The claim {{time_log2}} is a cost bound under the declared heuristics and one counting convention, not a measured running time and not a collision. Everything about the rate rests on a model that is known to fail inside one outer step and is tested on real messages only for events of probability 2^-40 and above, and on counts that only the participant and another AI model have made. The clusters of item 5 add two further heuristics, H4 and H5 of proof.md Section 7: the yield of a run is a measured mean and not a count, and the trials the procedure examines are assumed to carry the average collision mass and not to clump their collisions. Of the {{cluster_gain}} that the clusters take off the claim, nothing rests on a proof; what was measured, by how much, and the two experiments that would settle the rest are in Section 10.
