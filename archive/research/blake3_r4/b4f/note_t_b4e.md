## What this entry is

Our second entry for BLAKE3 reduced to 4 rounds (blake3-r4-prefix-v1). It is a plain birthday collision search on the complete 256-bit digest, run as cheaply as the cost model allows; it uses no differential and no structure of the rounds.
It is our own round-4 entry 41b32afc-ed4c-4ef1-b9f2-8c72ab163582 (41b32afc, 125.02375), which the judges rejected as not evaluable on one finding about the success-probability step of its Lemma 2 (see the next section). The program, the circuit, the recovery, the experiments and the declared 800-register machine are those of 41b32afc; this entry changes the success-probability proof (proof Section 4) and the plan constants that the new bound prices, and the score follows ({TL:.5f} instead of 125.02375). The memory statement of proof Section 3.6 also differs from 41b32afc: the sentence that gave the resident set of the Python reference generator and called it not charged is removed; the entry's generator is the C program measured in (i) and (ii) inside the 1 MiB cap, and the Python generator is named only as the reference whose output text the C program reproduces. No run of this entry is stated above the claimed peak memory. It is derived from our first round-4 entry 75d5e1f9-8948-4f49-b7a6-5e9475495a7a ({TL1}) with two changes (below). That entry is the program of our round-3 entry 62cfd777-5613-463f-b561-e2340e00e528 ({TL3}) taken to four rounds; the round-3 entry is a credited derivative of our own 0a9640ed-558a-4f0a-aba8-45e89ffcfd7c (124.96822) and 3356413f-cd60-410c-8847-2266d6444ac5 (124.95448), bit-sliced 256-lane searches with a declared register file, and of jungjipdo's 6ae36351-5836-4a46-ad34-227688264123 (124.91252), from whose proof text (3.3, 3.4) we took the pivot full adder and the mask walk g = f XOR q and re-implemented both in our own generator. We did not use or run any file of that entry. Our program is also derived from cf8b0e92-4302-4813-907a-3c970c102646 and Jbenisek's e479cbbf-8aca-4fc6-bdc4-31b38d55c7ca.
The target is the plain one: 64-byte messages 0^32 || x, one block, flags 11, standard IV, unkeyed, the complete digest. Against the three-round entry, four things changed in 75d5e1f9 (items 1, 2, 4 are unchanged here); two further things change in this entry (items 3 and 5):

1. Four rounds: the straight line has {PH} gates for 256 trails (17,238 at three rounds), the organizer's reference count is C(r4) = 846 operations (638 at three rounds), and the recovery circuit has {PH7} operations.
2. The program is larger ({instr} instructions of 36 bytes, {codebytes} bytes), so the table that fits under 2^20 bytes is small ({tmax} entries): the trails are long (L = 2^{log2L:.2f}) and the unfinished trails at the halt cost a larger share of the time than at three rounds. This is where the gap between this entry and the three-round figure comes from.
3. A table entry is 10 bytes (key 8, trail number 2). No trail length is stored (75d5e1f9 stored 16 bytes of length, 26 bytes per entry): the recovery recomputes both lengths in a first pass, and the recovery is charged at two passes of c + 2^48 batches (proof 3.4). The smaller entries give {tmax} table entries instead of 1,744; the trails become shorter (L = 2^{log2L:.2f} instead of 2^117.91), which also shortens the recovery, and the net gain is in the number below.
4. (unchanged from 75d5e1f9) The circuit generator was rewritten as two passes without a node table (the network has no shared and no unused gate), so that it fits next to the larger program; its code is charged at its compiled size and its memory was measured (below).

## Response to 41b32afc findings

The judges' single aggregate reason was `lane_cryptanalysis/heuristic/H4-finished-trail-birthday-conditioning`, from the finding F1-adaptive-finished-trail-conditioning: the step from a random lower bound U on the evaluations of finished trails to the fixed-U birthday product was "not justified", and "a proof of conservativeness or an explicit additional premise is required"; H4 was rated unsupported because no experiment or analysis isolated the conditioning claim. Both parts are answered. The other material findings, by id: F2-full-scale-random-function-extrapolation, F-SUCCESS-EXTRAPOLATION, F-EVAL-1, EXP-F1, EXP-F2 and EXP-F3 concern the extrapolation of H1 to width 256 and what the 18-bit and 21-bit program checks can show; they stand as declared (H1 limitations, no number estimated from a count, no new evidence claimed) and the sensitivity of the score to H1 is now priced in the note and proof Section 4. F-COST-REGISTERS (the 800-register convention, with its 512-register pricing in proof 3.5) is unchanged. F-COST-TIGHT-MEMORY is answered in point (4).
What changed. (1) Proof Section 4: the fixed-U step and the cut-deficit linearisation are gone. Lemma 2 now proves the bound for the random function by lazy sampling: given the history, a fresh evaluation equals any given earlier output with probability at least 2^-256, so the number of usable collisions has a predictable compensator and exp(S_t / N - (w - 1) H_t) w^R 1[no usable collision] is a supermartingale (usable: a new point against points of trails that finished before it, with the time and cut conditions written into the definition); a counting argument bounds S from below by pairs of evaluations minus explicit trail-length sums, whose Chernoff bounds are computed by the program; nothing is conditioned on which trails finish or on a random U. (2) Clause (d) of H1 declares the residual modelling step explicitly (the lazy-sampling hazard for the program's adaptive choices), with a sensitivity sentence; H2 shrinks to the cut count. (3) The counting also charges what the earlier lemma did not: a collision in the last batches of the run cannot finish its trail before the halt, and a collision with a point of a long trail needs that trail's length to finish. The program (bsrho.py, `_lossq`, `_b256`, `plan`) computes the exact bound with its own tools; it falls below 0.39 at the old batch count, so the plan is re-priced: batches B = {B}, Tp = {Tp} (was 68), age cap c = {kappa} L (was 11 L), one cut at most (was two), time_log2 {TL:.5f} (was 125.02375, +{dTL:.5f} bit), P_F bound {Pl:.5f}. (4) F-COST-TIGHT-MEMORY: the table has {tmax} entries (was 5,251) and the search phase leaves {spare} bytes under 2^20 (was 10); this costs nothing in the score at the printed precision.

## Premises, and what was checked

H1 (score-critical, model premise, four clauses): success of the attempt on the fixed target is at least the success P_F of the same procedure against a uniformly random function; clause (d) is the lazy-sampling hazard for the program's adaptive choices. It cannot be tested at width 256 and is declared, not proved: an assumption, no proof is known. Lemma 2 v2 proves P_F >= {Pcert} (exact certificate of Appendix A) for the random function with no premise besides H1; the cut-count heuristic H2 of the filed entry is withdrawn. A hazard deficit eps is repaired by extra time: {sens_short}. H3 (supporting, a declared machine convention): the 800-register machine convention, the 36-byte instruction charge, and the charge of the generator's code at 36 bytes per compiled instruction. Nothing else in the success bound is heuristic.
Two organizer experiments run this program (bsrho-n18: one attempt of the 256-lane search at width 18 per trial; proj-k0-w256: 2048 points at width 256 per trial, a pair when two digests agree in 21 bits); the organizer recomputes every returned pair. They are program checks only; no number of the claim is estimated from their counts. The program's own counts (`python3 bsrho.py --stats`, untrusted, descriptive): {stats1}; {stats2}.
Outside the shipped experiments, the program's scalar digest was compared with the organizer's reference `blake3(M, r)` at 3 rounds, at 4 rounds and at the full 7 rounds ({nchk} random messages each), and at 7 rounds also with the `blake3` library (2,000 messages): no differences. The bit-sliced step was compared with `blake3(M, 4)` on 8,192 messages and on 4,096 lanes chained for 4 steps, the packed recovery circuit on 448 messages: no differences. The selftest compares the step with the scalar compression on 4,096 lanes and on 4,096 lanes chained for 4 steps.

## Limits

- The full 256-bit functional graph, iterations beyond 2^40 and other keys were not tested; H1 is not proved.
- The 800-register file is this entry's declaration (the generated line needs 771); the profile fixes no register count. Smaller files are priced in proof 3.5 (512 registers: time_log2 {t512_note}; with 256 no table fits).
- The Python tooling (ledger, selftest, the reference checks, the organizer pipeline) ran under an address-space cap of 2 GB, not under a cap at the claimed 2^20 bytes: a Python interpreter cannot run there. Only the circuit generator, which defines the circuit, q, the register count and the instruction count, was run under 1 MiB caps. The 10-byte entry changes only the table, the recovery and the ledger; the program's counts for the experiments are identical to those of 75d5e1f9 (the same 175 of 256 attempts in the program's own width-18 statistics).
- The overlay of the generator's code and scratch on the table region is an assumption of the layout; the generator itself was run under the 1 MiB caps, but the machine of the cost model is not executed by any organizer check.
- The generator's code is charged by the number of WebAssembly instructions of the compiled C program ({gencode}), one instruction each; the machine of the model has no instruction set, so this count is a stated convention.
- Handler, key-scan and entry charges are stated upper bounds (proof 3.3); the generator is charged 200 operations per primitive call and per emitted line (its measured work in the sandbox is {genfuel} wasm operations against {genops} charged). Together they are below 1e-7 of the ledger.
